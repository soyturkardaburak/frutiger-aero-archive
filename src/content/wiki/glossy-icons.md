## A small object with a light source

The icons of the glossy years are little products. They have a light, usually from the upper left. They have a material, often glass, plastic, or a wet highlight. They often stand on a reflective ellipse, as if the desktop were a tabletop. Windows Vista pushed system icons to large sizes and asked them to survive that enlargement, which favored smooth gradients over the tiny pixel drawings of the 1990s. Transparent PNGs let a web page do a cheaper version of the same trick: an orb, a shadow, and a floor.

The sheet on this page was drawn for Aero Atlas. It is a study of the habit — globe, drop, leaf, sun, lens — and it is not a recreation of any company’s icon font. If you want the historical sets, the Guidebook Gallery and similar archives are the right rooms. They hold screenshots whose rights are not this site’s to redistribute.

## What the shine was for

A consistent light makes a toolbar feel like one photograph. That unity is the real craft, more than any single orb. Once every icon is lit from the same corner, a control panel looks designed rather than collected. Gloss also signaled resolution. A 16-pixel icon from 1998 cannot spend many pixels on a highlight. A 256-pixel icon can, and the highlight becomes proof that the computer is living in the larger size.

Web logos borrowed the floor without the toolbar. A wordmark, a reflection, a soft shadow: the entire identity as a product shot. It is easy to dismiss as a generator preset, because it often was. Presets become historically interesting when thousands of sites choose the same one.

## The flattening of the symbol

Flat design did not only remove gradients. It removed the tabletop. An icon became a sign again, closer to the airport pictogram that Frutiger’s letters had been drawn to accompany, and farther from a rendered object. That return is one reason the old orbs now look so specifically dated, and so easy to love. They are souvenirs of a moment when a folder could have a specular highlight and nobody thought that was too much.
