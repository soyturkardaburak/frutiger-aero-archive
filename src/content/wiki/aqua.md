## Water, before Aero

Apple showed Aqua at Macworld in January 2000. The public beta of Mac OS X followed in September, and version 10.0 shipped on 24 March 2001. The interface was the announcement that the Macintosh had a new operating system worth looking at: pinstriped window backgrounds, gel buttons, a traffic-light cluster of close, minimize, and zoom, and icons that looked licked. Steve Jobs talked about it as something you wanted to lick. The sentence is remembered because the chrome really did look edible.

CARI lists Aqua among alternate names associated with Frutiger Aero. That is a curator’s convenience. Aqua is earlier, it is Apple’s, and it belongs to the start of the decade rather than to the Vista years. It matters here because it made “wet” a respectable direction for a major desktop before Microsoft’s compositor existed.

## What it was made of

Classic Aqua is opaque in a way Aero Glass is not. The buttons look like drops, but you do not see the desktop blurred through the title bar. The pinstripes give the window a printed, almost textile grain. The icons sit in a perspective that later flattened. Over the years Apple sanded a lot of this down — brushed metal, then a quieter grey, long before iOS 7 — while the memory of the gel buttons remained the popular picture of “the old Mac OS X.”

Windows XP’s Luna theme, released in October 2001, is the contemporary answer from the other side of the industry: also colorful, also a break from grey bevels, also not yet glass. The glossy decade is a conversation between these desktops and the marketing around them, not a single product launch.

## Why the archive includes it

A history of Frutiger Aero that starts in 2007 starts too late. The water metaphor, the candy highlight, and the belief that a system theme could be delightful are all visible in Aqua. So is the risk of the later nickname: if every blue interface from 2000 to 2013 is forced into one label, Aqua loses its date and Aero loses its compositor. This article is the reminder. The illustration is a bowl of water, drawn for the archive, not a picture of the Macintosh.
