## The glass people actually lived with

If Vista introduced Aero, Windows 7 is the version that became furniture. It reached general availability on 22 October 2009. The major version number was the naming story — Vista had been 6.0 — and the design story was editorial. Microsoft kept the translucent frame and removed a lot of the feeling that the desktop was showing off. The palette is a little softer. The taskbar is taller, and programs pin to it instead of living mainly in a quick-launch strip. Window management grows three small verbs that are still easier to demonstrate than to describe: Snap a window to half the screen, Shake it to clear the others, Peek through the chrome to the desktop.

None of that is a new philosophy. It is Aero with the rough edges sanded, which is why nostalgia for “the glossy Windows” so often points at 7 even when the speaker thinks they mean Vista.

## A desktop that matched the advertising

By 2009 the surrounding commercial world had caught up with the theme. High-definition screens were ordinary enough that a wallpaper of sky and grass did not look like a luxury. Gadgets on the sidebar, reflective icons, and personal homepages with aqua gradients all sat comfortably next to the system chrome. The Waterloo exhibit treats this whole climate as one aesthetic. Windows 7 is simply the desktop where the climate felt finished rather than experimental.

It was also the last popular Windows whose default face was glass. Windows 8, three years later, would replace the Start menu with a flat full-screen field of tiles and begin the retirement of Aero as the identity of the product. People who missed 7 were often missing the material as much as the Start button.

## What not to confuse it with

Windows 7 did not invent gloss, water, or humanist type. Aqua was older. Web marketing had been reflective for several years. Early phones were busy imitating leather and paper. Windows 7 matters because it is the stable public reference for Aero Glass, the thing a later writer can point at and say: that, and the skies around it, are what the nickname is trying to hold. The drawing on this page is only a horizon. The system itself is in the history, not in the SVG.
