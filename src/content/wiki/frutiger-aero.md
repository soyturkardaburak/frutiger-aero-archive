## A name that arrived late

Frutiger Aero is a filing label for a look, not a brand that shipped on a box. The Consumer Aesthetics Research Institute coined it in 2017. The Institute’s own page describes a corporate technology aesthetic popular from about 2005 through 2013, and credits the name to Sofi Xian and Froyo Tam. Wiktionary records the coinage under Sofi Lee, another name used for the same researcher. The University of Waterloo Computer Museum, which later built an exhibit around the style, uses the span 2004–2013. The years wobble by a season. The point does not.

In the years the pictures were new, nobody needed the compound. A Windows user said Aero. A Macintosh user might still say Aqua, even though Aqua was older. A blogger said Web 2.0 and meant a gradient. The later phrase is useful because it notices that those habits shared a light: bright, wet, and convinced that the future would be legible.

## What the two words are doing

“Frutiger” is Adrian Frutiger’s humanist sans-serif, released in 1976 for the signs at Charles de Gaulle Airport. It is open, wide, and drawn for people who are walking. Later humanist faces — Myriad, and the Segoe UI that Windows Vista made ordinary — carry a family resemblance. The aesthetic’s name borrows that resemblance. It does not mean that every glossy advertisement set its headlines in Frutiger.

“Aero” is Microsoft’s. It is the glass desktop theme of Windows Vista and Windows 7. Microsoft expanded the word as Authentic, Energetic, Reflective, and Open. The expansion is a slogan. The visual fact is simpler: window frames became translucent, and the desktop started to look like a material instead of a painted bezel.

## What it looked like

CARI’s motif list is a good inventory: skeuomorphism, gloss, humanist type, glassy materials, aurora photographs, bokeh, and macro shots of grass. Waterloo adds round edges, high-definition pictures, and a three-dimensional look. Around those ingredients sits a particular optimism. Technology advertising of the period liked to put a device in weather — sky, water, lawn — as if the product had already arrived in a cleaner world. The archive’s guide to the aesthetic walks through the ingredients one by one.

The look was not only an operating-system theme. It was a commercial climate. Stock libraries, phone launch films, MP3-player packaging, and personal homepages all reached for the same horizon. A site like this one, built later, can imitate the furniture. It should not pretend the furniture was always called by one name.

## Why it faded, and why it came back

By 2012 and 2013 the large platforms had changed their minds in public. Windows 8 put flat tiles on the PC. iOS 7 removed a great deal of the textured realism from the phone. The glass and the grass began to look like the previous decade’s idea of the future, which is exactly when a style becomes available for nostalgia. The revival article on this site follows that second life. The important courtesy is to keep the dates in order: the pictures first, the nickname after.
