## A system people argued about, and a look they kept

Windows Vista reached consumers on 30 January 2007, after a development history that had been public, delayed, and partly restarted. During the Longhorn years the project had promised a great deal. What shipped was a new desktop compositor, a new security model that asked permission often, and a visual theme, Aero, that made qualifying PCs look unlike Windows XP. The reviews and the anecdotes tended to linger on slowness and on prompts. The glass lingered in memory for a different reason. It was the first time a default Windows desktop behaved like a sheet of material.

Microsoft announced that development was complete on 8 November 2006. The consumer date, two months later, is the one that matters for the aesthetic, because that is when the theme left the preview circuit and sat on ordinary desks — or failed to, when the graphics chip was not up to it.

## What changed on screen

Beside Luna, the XP theme, Vista’s Aero frames are quieter in hue and much more interested in blur. Title text sits on translucency. Buttons and icons grow larger and take on a consistent light from the upper left. Segoe UI replaces the older system face for much of the interface, which is one reason the later nickname reaches back to Frutiger: the new letters were humanist, open, and obviously not the compact UI face of the late 1990s.

Vista also offered plainer themes for machines that could not composite. The split mattered culturally. Aero was desirable and also a little exclusionary. A “Vista capable” sticker that did not mean “Aero capable” became part of the system’s bad reputation. The look and the hardware requirement were the same story.

## Why the disappointment does not erase the design

It is possible to think Vista was a poor release and still think the interface was the decade’s most influential piece of chrome. Windows 7, which people liked better, did not throw the glass away. It edited it. Personal sites, media-player skins, and gadget advertising spent the next several years echoing the same highlight, often without the compositor and often without the restraint. The encyclopedia article on Windows 7 picks up that second, more popular chapter.

When this archive draws a glassy window, the caption says it is a study. Vista’s own pixels are easy to find in screenshot galleries. They do not need to be copied into a fan site to be discussed.
