The name is younger than the feeling. Frutiger Aero was coined in 2017, by researchers who needed a shelf. Most of the people who now recognize the shelf were not using that phrase when the pictures were new. They were using the pictures. A wallpaper of sky. A phone that looked a bit like a pebble. A website with a gel button that said “enter.” The nostalgia starts when you notice that ordinary software no longer bothers to look delighted.

Delight is a risky word. It is also the right one. The glossy years were salesmanship, and they were sometimes tacky, and Vista could be a misery, and the meadows in the ads were not a climate policy. Underneath the salesmanship was a public style that expected technology to be cheerful. Cheerful is not the same as good. It is still a real quality, and it is scarce in a lot of later interface design, which prefers to look serious, neutral, and ready for a quarterly report.

I think the humanist letters matter more than the revival sometimes admits. Frutiger’s airport face, Segoe UI, Myriad, Calibri: these are letters that assume a person is reading, not a brand that is posing. Paired with glass and grass, they made the machine feel like a public place rather than a cockpit. Flat design kept some of the humanist skeletons and drained the weather. What people miss, when they miss “Frutiger Aero,” is the weather.

There is a duty that comes with building an archive about a nickname. You say when the nickname started. You do not invent a scene in which everyone in 2008 already spoke it. You credit the Institute that did the naming, and the museum that took the style seriously, and you draw your own bubbles instead of stealing a campaign. After that, you are allowed to say the simple thing. The future in those pictures looked happy to see you. A lot of us would like a future that still is.
