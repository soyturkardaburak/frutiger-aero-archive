In 2008 a personal site that wanted to look current had a recipe. The page sat in a column, often near a thousand pixels, because that was a polite width on a 1024 or 1280 screen and because the author had built the layout in a table or in a careful float. Above the column was a banner with a picture in it. Under that, a row of buttons with a highlight along the top, because a button that did not shine looked like a leftover from a frameset. The background, if the author was in a good mood, was a sky.

None of this was quoting the past. It was the present tense of enthusiasm. Browsers could finally be trusted with a bit of transparency. Screens were wide enough for a header illustration. The word “Web 2.0” was still being used in earnest by people who had read, or had heard about, Tim O’Reilly’s essay from the autumn of 2005. The essay is about participation. The sites were about looking as if participation would be pleasant.

I am writing this in 2026, on a site that borrows the recipe on purpose. The borrow is the subject, not a disguise. Aero Atlas is not a recovered 2008 homepage. It is a later encyclopedia wearing the furniture, and the furniture is loud enough that I would rather say so in the first post than in a footnote. The dates on these essays are the dates I wrote them. Backdating them would turn the costume into a fib.

What I want the furniture to do is narrow the distance. A flat, airy layout with huge type would talk about Frutiger Aero from across the room. A beveled title bar talks about it from inside the habit. The risk is pastiche, the kind that remembers only the gradients and forgets that those pages were full of text, badges, link lists, and a slightly too-small font. The encyclopedia is an attempt to put the text back. The sky can stay. It was never the part that needed defending.
