Skeuomorphism left in a way gloss did not. Gloss wore out. You can watch it thin from Vista to Windows 7 to the early flat tiles, a highlight growing embarrassed. The stitched leather and the yellow paper were escorted out. iOS 7, released on 18 September 2013, is the day I use when I need a single date, because Apple’s phone had been the most committed mainstream classroom for those metaphors. Windows 8 had already told the PC, the year before, that tiles and type would do the teaching from now on.

I understood the argument immediately and resented it anyway. The argument was that the metaphors had become baby talk. People knew what a calendar was. Drawing stitches around it was a kind of nostalgia inside the present, which is an odd thing for a new operating system to ship. The resentment was about texture. A flat list is efficient and it is also a little contemptuous of the hand, as if looking at a material were a waste of a tap. Both feelings can be true. Efficiency is not the same virtue as warmth, and warmth is not an excuse for a slow metaphor.

What got lost in the popular telling is the difference between a metaphor and a finish. A glossy Aero frame is barely a metaphor. It is glass because the compositor can do glass, and because glass looked like the future. A Notes application made of paper is a metaphor with a job: it tells you the program stores scraps. When the revival piles them on one moodboard labeled Frutiger Aero, I want a card in the corner that says these were different decisions. CARI’s definition includes skeuomorphism. Fine. Inclusion is not sameness.

I do not want the leather back as a default. I want the right to notice that a decade of interfaces trusted objects, and that the trust ended on a published schedule. Disappearance is too soft a word. It was a redesign, and it was meant to be noticed.
